Windows & doors

Installation, replacement and repair for homes and commercial premises across Limerick and Kerry.

Old windows are expensive to keep

Failed or ageing units are one of the largest sources of heat loss in an Irish property, and one of the most obvious to live with - draughts, condensation between the panes, rooms that never quite warm up, and rattling every time the wind gets up.

Not everything needs replacing

Failed locks, worn handles, dropped or misaligned sashes and perished seals are frequently repairable at a fraction of replacement cost. Replacing a whole unit because a mechanism failed is a common and expensive mistake.

Security is the quiet half of this

Locks and handles are the part of a window most likely to be tested, and among the cheapest things to upgrade.

Signs you need this

  • Condensation forming between the panes rather than on them
  • A noticeable draught with the window fully closed
  • The handle or lock no longer engages cleanly
  • The sash has dropped and catches on the frame
  • Timber frames soft or discoloured at the bottom corners

When you don't need us

If the only fault is a stiff handle, a failed lock or a draught around one frame, do not let anyone sell you a replacement window. Those are repairs, and we would rather do the repair than the replacement.

Questions

Straight answers.

Can you repair instead of replacing?

Very often, yes. Locks, handles, hinges, seals and alignment are usually repairable, and we will tell you when that is the better value option.

Do you fit double and triple glazing?

Yes, for domestic and commercial properties.

How disruptive is a replacement?

Most domestic jobs are a day or two depending on the number of units, and we clear up as we go rather than at the end.

Are there grants for this?

Home energy grant schemes exist in Ireland and eligibility depends on the property and the works. We will tell you what appears to apply to your situation and point you at the official scheme - we do not administer grants.

Do you cover Kerry?

Yes, Limerick and Kerry.
